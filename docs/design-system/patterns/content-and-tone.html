Pattern

Content and tone

Hewn talks like a calm operator: what happened, what it means for your work, and what you can do, with real names and real numbers.

Overview

The rules of record (casing, punctuation, number formats, the error and empty formulas) are in Content. This page shows them applied across components, with the words the mocks already use, so new screens sound like the old ones.

Voice

  • Calm. Hewn runs unattended and spends money; people open Mission Control worried. Lead with what is safe: Agents keep working; nothing is lost.
  • Specific. Name the project, the worker, the stage, the code: Hewn’s GitHub App can’t write to northwind/handover. Never an error occurred.
  • Plain. Describe what the factory does in its own terms (stage, increment, worker) and explain them in empty states rather than avoiding them.
  • Brief. One sentence for the condition, one for the consequence or the fix. Dialogs may take three when money or history is at stake.
  • Neutral about people. Say who did it, never blame: Paused by quinntynebrown at 14:29; Ask an Owner to change your role.

When to use

MomentToneExample
Routine newsMatter of fact, no adjectivesTidewater started stage 3
SuccessState the result and what did not change; no exclamation marksDefaults saved. Directions already running keep theirs.
WarningSay the threshold and what happens at the limit$200.00 of $250.00. It pauses at $250.00.
FailureSource and code, what is safe, the next stepThe API returned 503. Nothing was created; your text is kept below. Try again.
Destructive confirmationExact consequence, what is kept, irreversibility…deleted from Hewn. This can’t be undone. The GitHub repository is kept.
Refusal (role)The reason for the rule and who can helpViewers can watch every project but can’t give direction. Ask an Owner to change your role.

Button verbs

  • Verb + object, sentence case, up to three words: Start direction, Save defaults, Add a GitHub login, Copy token.
  • The confirm button repeats the title's verb: Stop all workers? → Stop all workers.
  • Busy labels use the progressive form and the ellipsis character: Saving…, Revoking…, Stopping 3 workers….
  • Two outcomes in one press are named together: Raise and resume.

Responsive behaviour

  • Write for 360px first: button labels never wrap; if one does, shorten it (Raise and resume, not Raise the budget and resume the direction).
  • Secondary facts may hide below lg but must not be the only place a fact lives: Last sign-in is .lg-only, and the row still makes sense without it.
  • Truncate names with an ellipsis only in rows and the sidebar, where the full name is one click away; never truncate status words, money or errors.
  • Long identifiers (repository URLs, SHAs) are mono and may break anywhere (overflow-wrap: anywhere), rather than pushing the layout wide.

Accessibility

Plain language

Short sentences, common words, one idea each. Factory terms (stage, increment, worker) are explained the first time a person meets them, in the empty state of the page that shows them.

Names

  • Accessible names include the object when the visible label does not: Archive Ledgerline, Remove ama-osei, Dismiss: Parcel Post is blocked.
  • An aria-label starts with the visible text (2.5.3 Label in Name): the button reading Restore is named Restore Saltmarsh Survey.
  • Links say where they go: Open log, Go to projects. Never here or more.
  • Visually hidden text completes what icons say: Ledgerline, Running, 62% in the sidebar.

Status in words

Every status has a word; colour and icon only reinforce it (1.4.1). Severity is in the words too: blocked, can’t be undone, nothing is lost.

Touch

Short labels keep targets wide enough to hit without crowding: three-word buttons fit two to a row at 360px.

Content

  • Sentence case everywhere: titles, buttons, tabs, labels, column headers. Capitalise only names (Ledgerline, GitHub, Magic Patterns) and the roles Owner and Viewer.
  • Curly apostrophes and quotes (couldn’t, Kiln Booking’s), the ellipsis character (…), the middle dot (·) between facts, the em dash (—) for an absent value.
  • Contractions are fine and preferred: can’t, don’t, wasn’t.
  • No please, sorry, oops, successfully or exclamation marks. No simply or just.
  • Second person for the reader (your directions), Hewn by name for the system (Hewn runs to merged code), people by login (quinntynebrown).
  • One word per concept: use the microcopy library and glossary above; a new screen that needs a new verb adds it there first.
